Movement here isn't a new capability — it's an application of two you
already have. Week 2's hold-duration can map onto jump height; week 6's
momentum can map onto running speed. Week 7's mode-switching decides which
mapping is live when. The auto-run-plus-jump-height shape is the obvious
version; the exercise asks you to justify your own choice of mapping instead
of defaulting to it.

## Before the session

Bring your week 2 (hold) and week 6 (momentum) prototypes.

## In the session

Demo of the auto-run/jump-height shape as one worked example, then build
time on your own spatial mapping.

## Studio exercise

Build a prototype where the single button controls both when something
moves and how far or fast, using a continuous signal you've already built
(hold-duration or momentum). Do not add a second input axis to make this
easier — if you need one, the mapping is wrong, not the constraint.

## By the end

You can describe movement in this course as spatial mapping of an existing
signal, not as a new input capability in its own right.
