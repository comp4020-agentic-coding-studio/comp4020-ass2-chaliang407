Every week so far has read timing that the player controls. Scanning inverts
that: an external cursor cycles through options at a fixed cadence, and the
button's only job is to confirm when it's on the right one. This is week 7's
mode-dependent meaning again — but now the "mode" is decided by an automatic
clock instead of the player, which is the same shape as week 4's rhythm run
in reverse. It's also how real single-switch assistive-access interfaces
work, not an invented constraint for this course.

## Before the session

Bring a prototype that uses sequence or movement — you'll be replacing its
timing model, not its content.

## In the session

Demo of a working scanning-cursor prototype, then build time adapting your
own, followed by playtesting each other's under a constraint (reduced
reaction time, or looking away from the screen).

## Studio exercise

Adapt an earlier prototype to be playable through an auto-scanning cursor
plus a single confirm press. Play it under a real constraint yourself, and
write down — specifically, not generally — what broke and why.

## By the end

You understand that this semester's one-button constraint is not a
game-jam gimmick: it's the literal access method some real users have to
any software at all, not only games.
