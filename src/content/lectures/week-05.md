A vocabulary doesn't need new hardware — weeks 2 and 3 already gave you two
distinguishable symbols, short and long, and week 4 already gave you a way
to tell where one gesture ends and the next begins. Sequence is what happens
once you stop treating those as separate mechanics and start treating them
as an alphabet: symbols, read in a row, standing for something a single
press never could.

The design constraint worth naming is size. A one- or two-symbol alphabet
barely counts as a vocabulary; a fifteen-symbol one asks more of a player's
memory than a one-button game usually should. Three to five symbols sits in
a working range not because of any rule specific to single-button design,
but because it's close to what most players can hold in short-term memory
while also paying attention to whatever the sequence controls.

## Outline

- sequence as a use of press-shapes you already have, not a new signal in
  its own right
- alphabet size as a genuine trade-off: too small isn't a vocabulary, too
  large asks too much of memory
- what a segmented stream of symbols can represent that one symbol alone
  can't
