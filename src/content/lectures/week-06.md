Every mechanic so far resets the instant a gesture ends — the next press
starts from zero. Momentum is the semester's first mechanic that doesn't,
and that changes what "playing well" means: it stops being about any single
press and becomes about not stopping, which is a different kind of skill
from anything weeks 1–5 asked for.

It's worth being precise about what momentum isn't: a score. A score also
persists, but it never punishes inaction — it just waits. Momentum has to
decay on its own, unprompted, or it collapses into being a score with a
different name. Decay rate is the one number that decides whether the
mechanic feels tense (fast decay, constant pressure) or forgiving (slow
decay, occasional lapses tolerated) — the first place this semester where a
single tuning constant changes a prototype's emotional register, not just
its scoring.

## Outline

- why persistent, decaying state asks for a different kind of skill than
  anything in weeks 1–5
- momentum versus a score: the difference is that momentum has to punish
  stopping, not just remember
- decay rate as a tuning constant that changes how a mechanic feels, not
  only how it scores
