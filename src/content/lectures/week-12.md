Every studio session this semester ended with something new to press. This
one doesn't, and that absence is deliberate: the skill being tested now is
scope, not addition. Twelve weeks of vocabulary is more than any single game
needs, and knowing that — rather than trying to prove otherwise — is what
separates a finished game from an inventory of mechanics.

There's a difference worth drawing between cutting something because you
ran out of time and cutting it because it didn't serve the game. Both leave
the same gap in the final build, but only one of them is a decision anyone
else can learn from. The design note this week asks for is evidence of the
second kind: not just what got left out, but why leaving it out made the
game better, specifically — a harder claim to support than "there wasn't
time."

## Outline

- why the final week adds no mechanic, and what skill that absence is
  actually testing
- cutting for focus versus cutting for time: the same gap in the build, but
  only one is a defensible decision
- what a scope decision has to say to count as deliberate, not merely
  convenient
