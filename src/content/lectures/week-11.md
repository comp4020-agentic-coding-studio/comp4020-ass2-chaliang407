Scanning inverts everything so far: an external cursor keeps time and cycles
through options at a fixed cadence, and the button's only job is to confirm
when it lands on the right one. It's week 7's mode-switching again, but now
the mode is chosen by a clock instead of the player — and it's not a
hypothetical: this is how real single-switch assistive-access interfaces
work, for people whose only input to any software is one switch.

## Outline

- scanning as externally-timed confirmation, the inverse of week 4's
  player-timed rhythm
- why this is the same "one input, several meanings" idea as week 7, with
  the clock moved outside the player
- what a semester of one-button design reveals about accessible input in
  general, not just about games
