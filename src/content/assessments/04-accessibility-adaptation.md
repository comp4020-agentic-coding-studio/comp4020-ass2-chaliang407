> Take a prototype built for direct, player-timed input, and make it work
> for someone whose only input is a single, well-timed confirm.

The adaptation doesn't need to be elegant — it needs to work, and the
account of what didn't survive the change needs to be specific enough that
someone who hasn't seen your prototype could still learn something from it.

## What you submit

The adapted prototype, and a written account (no fixed length, but vague
answers like "it was harder to play" will be marked down) of exactly what
broke and what you changed to fix or accept it.
