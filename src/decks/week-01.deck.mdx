# Press

How much interaction can one button contain?

---

## The hardware budget

One input. Two states: down, not-down. Plus timing — when it went down, when
it came back up, how long between one and the next.

That's the entire budget for this semester. Nothing else gets added to it.

---

## Most things assume more

Almost every interactive system you've used assumes at least a pair of
inputs: one to commit, a second to back out or correct course.

This course removes the second one. On purpose.

---

{/* _class: banner */}

**What's left, when there's only one?**

---

## A bicycle bell

Squeeze it: one ring. Squeeze it again: another ring. The bell does not
remember the last squeeze, and it does not stay ringing after your hand
leaves it. Each squeeze is its own, separate occurrence.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

A press is not a state you're in. It's something that already finished
happening.

---

## Why that distinction matters here

Every later week in this course adds something to the plain press — a
duration, a release, a rhythm, a memory of earlier presses. All of it sits
on top of the same fact: the press itself is instantaneous and leaves nothing
behind on its own.

Get this wrong early and every later week gets harder to reason about, not
easier.
